# Exact planar cloth contact: mechanism and bounded corrections

The original 128² driven S1 case remains **rejected at its original 10% axial strain limit**. The shared trigger is an invariant coplanar input. Jolt's last contact projection adds a directly observed local compression residual; XPBD has a separate float position reconstruction error. Neither backend has qualified a general cloth contact solution. Both experimental triangle-contact implementations do close a demonstrated vertex-sampling gap on a bounded set of sphere contacts. The XPBD double state also fixes the separately measured momentum error on its predeclared controls.

## The original failure

The frozen case uses an 8 m XY cloth, fixed top edge, in-plane gravity and a +2 m/s launch. A 1 m sphere with a 0.05 m effective cloth shell moves from x=5.2 to x=4 over 0.66 s at z=0 by static pose assignment. The original Jolt trace is `b9cc9db786d0843550c1e6fab1b2bcea457784d3f58b780caadb76b861ce759f` (SHA-256). It reaches 44.44% tension and 68.03% compression; the frozen XPBD High32 reaches 42.66% and 36.79%. Every published Z is exactly zero. The [earlier source trace and control table](ContactFailureDiagnosis.md) records this failure before the experimental changes and reproduces it through the original provider path. The configurable corpus preserves the authored motion, but its pose arithmetic orders float operations differently. The frozen XPBD replay analysis traced a few one-ULP pose differences to that change, so only the original provider path is the bit-exact original reproducer.

In a reflection-equivariant update, `R(q)=q` for a state on the plane implies `R(F(q))=F(q)`. Radial vertex normals, structural distance corrections, pin targets and authored body motion have no transverse component in the frozen implementations. Both solvers therefore remain in the planar subspace. Opposite ±10 µm and ±1 cm inputs select reflected 3D branches without changing the material; their full trajectory comparisons discriminate the missing transverse direction from a weaker load. Original offset runs reach roughly 6% axial strain, which establishes only that metric. No experiment adds random jitter or changes the original gate.

The first native axial failure is frame index 6. The triangulated disk is still injective at that frame. At frame index 32 adjacent faces 13460 and 13459 first have overlapping interiors across edge (6782,6910); there are 124 inverted faces at frame 39. Thus overlap is a later symptom. At frame 6, moving free node 8318 a small distance in either Z direction keeps the pins and cannot worsen sphere clearance. Its transverse spring surrogate curvature is −417,397 J/m²; a ±0.1 mm displacement lowers the surrogate energy by 0.002087 J. This identifies a real transverse descent direction, not an energy audit of the time integrator. See [triangle evidence](../ContactQualification/OriginalPlanarTriangleFinding.md) and the [transverse calculation](../ContactQualification/TransverseMode.md).

The original input does **not** prove an impossible planar geometry. A continuous affine shear can clear the driven sphere with fixed top pins, positive triangle areas and only 3.634% axial strain. Its diagonal strain is about 14.41%, and the reconstructed spring energy is about 2.380 MJ versus 0.238 MJ in the observed final planar state. This is a kinematic certificate, not a physical solution of the original initial value problem or a lower bound on planar elastic energy. The demonstrated limitation is that the tested deterministic local solvers, with the authored dynamics and existing constraints, do not select the low-strain 3D response from this input. See [feasibility](../ContactQualification/PlanarFeasibility.md) and the [energy comparison](../ContactQualification/PlanarEnergyComparison.md).

## Ordering, geometry and prescribed motion

The same native edge (6526,6527) changes from −48.02% to −97.53% strain inside `ApplyCollisionConstraintsAndUpdateVelocities` at integration 2720, after the final edge sweep. No elastic sweep follows that projection in the frozen integration. This is source evidence of a local residual; it does not show that the full step creates net physical energy.

| Native order | Change from frozen A | Peak tension / compression, original planar case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Predict, solve edges, apply final vertex projection and velocity response | 44.44% / 68.03% |
| B | Project contact before the structural finish | 52.54% / 64.99% |
| C1 / C4 | Alternate projected contact and structural repair, one or four passes | 37.55% / 38.04%; 35.85% / 38.32% |
| D4 | Unilateral contact multiplier in the alternating prototype | 35.85% / 38.32% |
| E | Refresh tangent contact geometry | 44.45% / 68.03% |

Every row stays planar and fails. Alternation removes the observed late inversions in the C traces but still fails axial strain. Early projection can also exhaust the later penetration test and omit friction/support; F retains unilateral support for final velocity response and still reaches about 38% compression. In the last 60 frames of the 240-frame control, A retains about 64% compression and C/D about 39%. Their peak compression varies by at most 0.0061 percentage points and measured kinetic energy stays below 0.087 J. The persistent error is a settled, loaded planar state, not just an oscillating startup transient. `SettlingTailReduction.json` records the windows and corrects an old reduction's hard-coded displayed frame count; the old result is retained. The XPBD ordering alternatives likewise leave its original axial failure at about 42.6%. More iterations, higher stiffness, longer settling and replacing static jumps with true kinematic motion each failed to resolve the planar branch. True kinematic motion reduces the frozen native compression to 52.18%; the distinction includes changed temporal sampling and must not be attributed to relative velocity alone.

Native G isolates prescribed surface velocity in the non-dynamic vertex path. It resolves only approaching normal relative velocity and uses relative tangent motion for friction. Its tests cover moving surfaces, separation and static parity. XPBD G separately derives point velocity from the prescribed translating sphere's exact substep center difference and updates the previous position to preserve the resolved velocity. It does not advertise reciprocal dynamic coupling. A preliminary control with a kinematic flag but no `MoveKinematic` retained the old pose path; its raw output and corrected interpretation are preserved. The configured corpus records actual native collider state and distinguishes XPBD's prescribed/static semantics.

## Correcting the surface gap

Vertex-only contacts cannot see a sphere crossing a triangle interior or edge between samples. The 9² face/edge cases start with adequate clearance and reach 250 mm triangle-shell penetration with the frozen path. The isolated Jolt H and XPBD E branches use the closest point on the triangle, its barycentric coordinates, and weights `sum(w_i b_i²)` for movable vertex inverse masses `w_i`. The exact zero-distance tie uses oriented face geometry, with no fixture jitter. XPBD handles degenerate segments in its closest-point calculation. Native I adds unilateral point velocity and bounded friction; it keeps the frozen path for unsupported collision settings. It separates movable cloth impulse, computed pin reaction and prescribed body reaction. For that velocity-only operation `ΔK − J·V_surface = J·v_rel + 0.5 W |J|² ≤ 0` under the tested response assumptions. This does not account for positional projection or elastic work. Scope is sphere versus cloth with prescribed/static rigid motion and supported response settings; generic dynamic face response is not qualified.

| Fixture | Frozen Jolt penetration | Jolt IG penetration | Jolt IG peak axial |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 9² face center | 250 mm | 0 | 0.051% |
| 9² edge midpoint | 250 mm | 0 | 0.059% |
| 9² corner vertex | 2.783 mm | 0 | 0.086% |
| Small planar ±10 µm offsets | 61.32 mm | 5.60 µm | 1.865% |
| Exact original 128² | 0.800 mm | 2.63 µm | **68.065%** |

XPBD ES likewise passes the small face/edge/corner geometry checks, with at most 0.042 µm measured shell penetration in the three fixtures. In the exact original it reduces peak penetration to about 1.93 µm while still reaching **42.68%** axial strain with no planar triangle flips. The signed 128² ES offsets reach about 5.53% axial with about 2.10 µm penetration. The separately tested GS signed offsets reach about 6.07% axial and 0.952 mm penetration, close to the separate 1 mm gate, and fail some small sphere-contact geometry fixtures. Native IG leaves the exact 128² case at Z=0 and records 582 projected flip instances there. The geometry correction does not choose a transverse mode for that case. Full 3D self-contact, deformation energy, external work and reciprocal dynamic face response remain incomplete; geometry passes are not blanket physical passes.

Projected flips in an offset 3D trace do not establish a physical intersection. The [independent 3D probe](../ContactQualification/Nonlocal3DSelectedFrameFinding.md) checked all 32,258 faces on three selected late frames each of the 128² ES and fast IG +10 µm traces. It found no clear interior edge crossing between faces with no shared vertices among 66,248 overlapping candidate pairs. This includes each trace's frame with the largest projected flip count. A separate nine-case small XPBD E check found no intersection among disjoint faces over 540 sampled frames. Neither check certifies adjacent folds, a continuous trajectory, or the rest of the large trace.

## XPBD precision correction

The actual XPBD source ledger measures both equal-and-opposite intended edge corrections and the rounded float position deltas. Independently rounded stores create a free-free momentum term. Dividing their position error by the smaller substep amplifies it: raw residual grows at 16, 32 and 64 substeps, while subtracting the *measured* numerical term reduces the residual maxima by 21.57–112.41×. That subtraction is diagnosis, not a solver correction. The first ideal reconstruction variant substantially improves aggregate momentum but introduces 5.4–6.5 mm/s local velocity jitter in uniform flight and fails its analytic gate.

Mode 2 keeps double positions, previous positions, rest lengths and multipliers in the experimental XPBD structural solve. It exchanges actual changes with the existing float contact path, keeps pin targets absolute, and publishes the existing float interface. It applies no global momentum adjustment. Across nine uniform-flight cases at N16/32/64, it has zero published local velocity, momentum and kinetic error and at most 0.235 µm position error. Across all 27 predeclared contact and pinned controls it passes the 10 ppm direct raw cloth momentum gate; worst contact is 1.96 ppm and worst pin is 0.0782 ppm. Moving controls still have 18–28 J of unclosed full-step energy residual. This is a verified numerical improvement in those scopes, not full energy closure or a fix for the exact planar strain. See the [precision ledger review](../ContactQualification/PrecisionSurfaceIndependentReview.md), [fixed bounds](../ContactQualification/FreeFlightGateProtocol.md) and [independent double-state review](../ContactQualification/ShadowControlsIndependentReview.md).

## Matched work and performance

The face pass initially adds a full serial triangle scan to Jolt's final constraint group. `PhysicsSystem.cpp`'s soft simulation jobs retry or yield until the group is done, so waiting workers can raise process CPU without increasing useful cloth work. The scalar double-precision AABB experiment (cull 1) retained trajectory parity but increased clean P50 from about 112 to 415 ms and process CPU from about 35 to 127 s. It is a retained negative. Cull 2 tests inexpensive vector min/max bounds against an outward-expanded sphere cube before closest-point work, refreshes the bound when corrected vertices expand its finite scale, and falls back to the full path on invalid bounds. An independent review rehashed the 52 compressed trajectory files, checked all 2,880 per-frame position/velocity digests and re-packed 520 raw arrays. The whole trace is byte identical for 26 cases and 1,440 frames. Geometric containment does not turn the float guard into a guarantee on every ill-conditioned mesh.

The table uses the 40-frame, 16,384-vertex +10 µm fixture, worker cap 8, one Ryzen 9 5900X Windows/MSVC worker, the same binary per backend comparison, tracing/accounting/native profiling off. Values are medians of three counterbalanced runs' percentiles. Allocation counts and requested bytes come from separate instrumentation of C++ `new` and Jolt/Box3D hooks over admission plus simulation, not every process allocation. Clean state hashes agree with separate full trace runs. The two Jolt cull settings produce exactly the same full position/velocity trajectories and geometry verdict. The XPBD settings both pass geometry on this fixture, with different trajectories; the comparison measures the cost of precision, not a matched-state speedup or a backend ranking.

`Worker16KParity.json` additionally compares the local one- and eight-worker full-state frame hash lists on the 16K fixture. IG with fast culling is bit exact at the same Engine and native source; the archived mode-0 IG and ES streams likewise match their earlier one-worker captures across the recorded source checkpoints.

Fast culling saves 31.1% P50, 34.0% P99 and 30.4% process CPU in this Jolt case without changing allocations. Mean process CPU equivalents stay about 7.75–7.77; that measure includes retries/waits and does not represent useful worker utilization. XPBD double state costs 11.2% P50 and about 2.73 MB more tracked requests. XPBD reports eight workers but one body job per substep (1,280 total), about 12.48% of its worker window and roughly one process CPU equivalent. These are workload-specific costs. `MatchedTiming.json` retains all samples and the fixture verdicts; `Cull-20261109T114335-*` retains the rejected scalar cull.

In a separate scope build with identical state hashes, Jolt's serial face work falls from 47.83 to 13.95 ms per frame. The cull rejects 96.72 million of 103.23 million face candidates across 3,200 integrations. Edge work stays about 241 ms summed across workers per frame, and barrier work falls from 111.41 to 77.16 ms. The XPBD counters move from 6.88 to 11.09 ms/frame for integration and from 333.06 to 352.91 for constraints; collision is about 206–207 ms/frame. These are nested stage sums and may overlap. They are neither wall time partitions nor a claim that one backend is faster at the same physical quality. `MatchedProfiles.json` keeps all counts and exact source IDs.

The same native diagnostic records the isolated face velocity ledger on two actually moving kinematic spheres: a face center and a face near the fixed top row. Computed impulse balance residual is at most 2.96e−7 N·s per component, the pinned probe has a nonzero 3.32 N·s peak pin reaction component, and `ΔK − J·V_surface` is nonpositive in every recorded correction integration. These sums validate the signs and bookkeeping for the tested velocity operation; the substantial actuator work in the pinned case is not a full energy balance. The face-position projection, structural energy, static pose work and reciprocal dynamic contact remain separate open quantities in the broader model.

This demonstrates a benchmark input difference at onset, and explains why frame-end collider metadata alone did not reveal it. It is not proof of exact solver replay for bit-identical repeated inputs. Treat this waveform as a cross-platform trajectory exception, while keeping its geometry decisions and raw divergence. Exact cross-platform replay of such fixtures requires a shared bit-stable substep input stream and a separate replay check.

Keep the exact planar case as a negative control at 10%. Where a low-strain wrap is the intended test, specify a physically authored out-of-plane load or geometry and a reflected counterpart. Keep strain, triangle-shell clearance, finite full trajectories, pin drift and scope-specific impulse/work checks separate. The corrections here qualify bounded geometry, velocity and numerical behaviors in the experiments. They do not justify promoting a general contact backend or changing production defaults. PR #20 remains draft and production physics is unchanged. Development priority returns to rendering, textures, materials and PBR.
